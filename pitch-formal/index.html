Executive Summary

Grid-Pulse NTL is a business-to-government venture that recovers stolen electricity for distribution companies. A low-cost sensor on each distribution transformer measures the energy leaving the transformer; the platform compares it against registered consumption and an AI-predicted technical (natural) loss, and reports the unexplained excess as suspected theft — with a probability, a reasons checklist, and an estimate of the kilowatts at risk.

The commercial model aligns the company with the utility: we take a share of the energy we help recover, on top of a modest hardware and platform fee. At typical recovery rates a single node pays for itself in under two months. The system is built and demonstrated; this proposal seeks EGP 30 million in seed capital to fund a district pilot and reach operating profitability.

The differentiator is not measurement — it is attribution. An AI baseline predicts how much loss is normal for the present heat and load, so only the genuinely unexplained remainder is flagged. This removes the false alarms that make simple meter-difference methods unusable in the Egyptian summer.
2

The Problem: Non-Technical Loss

Distribution companies lose energy in two ways. Technical loss is physical — heat in cables and transformers — and it rises with temperature and load. Non-technical loss (NTL) is theft: illegal direct hooks, tampered meters, and unmetered workshops and kilns. In Egypt, NTL is estimated at EGP 25–35 billion per year, and it worsens load-shedding by overloading transformers with demand no one is billed for.

The operational difficulty is that both losses present identically at the transformer: as "more loss." A fixed threshold on measured loss raises alarms on every hot afternoon and is therefore ignored. Utilities are left with manual, tip-driven inspections that are slow, costly, and unrepeatable.

3

The Solution

Grid-Pulse runs a continuous energy balance at each transformer — energy measured leaving the transformer, less registered consumption. It then predicts the expected technical loss for the current conditions using a multi-factor model, and treats the difference as the candidate theft signal. That signal is confirmed or dampened by three independent lines of evidence — harmonic signature, power factor, and a longer-horizon energy accounting — before a verdict is issued.

Every verdict is explainable: a theft probability, a checklist of which indicators fired, an estimate of the kilowatts unaccounted for, and a recommended action. Field-inspection teams receive ranked, localized, time-stamped leads rather than raw numbers.

4

Technology & Detection

Detection is a fusion of independent layers, each contributing evidence to a single explainable verdict. No layer raises an alarm on its own.

  • Energy balance — measured transformer output less registered meter consumption.
  • AI technical-loss baseline — a Random-Forest regression over seven features (load, temperature, hour, day, voltage, current, transformer rating) predicts the natural loss; the residual is what matters.
  • Harmonic signature (1D-CNN) — a convolutional classifier separates a clean waveform, a legitimate industrial signature, and an illegal-bypass signature, so a registered high-distortion factory is not mistaken for theft.
  • Transformer energy accounting — an independent layer accumulates incoming versus registered energy over a horizon and reports the excess loss as corroborating or dissenting evidence.
  • Temporal anomaly — an Isolation-Forest flags consumption patterns inconsistent with the transformer's history.
  • Decision fusion & explainability — the layers combine into a calibrated theft probability with a reasons checklist.

5

Field Deployment

The field target is a sealed distribution kiosk (ring-main unit). Grid-Pulse retrofits it non-invasively, on the low-voltage side only: three Rogowski coils are clipped around the busbars, feeding an edge node in a weatherproof enclosure, with the antenna routed outside the metal shell. The medium-voltage chamber is never entered; no conductor is cut; no feeder is disconnected; and the installation is fully reversible.

Production sensing uses Rogowski coils (rated to several thousand amperes), an IP65 enclosure, and LoRaWAN or cellular backhaul — a design suitable for national rollout across an estimated 230,000 distribution transformers.

Market & Competitive Position

Utilities fight theft today by manual audit, by full smart-meter rollout, by analytics on meter data, by feeder monitoring, or by fixed-threshold loss alarms. Each is either slow, blind to cause, or dependent on a metering estate that costs billions to build.

Grid-Pulse occupies the position none of these hold: low capital cost, explainable, and effective on a grid that was never fully metered. One node observes an entire feeder of customers — a fraction of per-meter cost — and the AI baseline removes the false positives that make cheaper analytics unusable. Each verdict adds labelled theft data, a compounding advantage a fixed-threshold competitor cannot replicate.

The Investment Ask

Grid-Pulse seeks EGP 30 million (approximately USD 625,000) in seed capital to carry the company through a district pilot and two years to the Year-3 EBITDA turn. Use of funds: the field pilot (1,000 nodes and gateways, 40%), product and machine-learning engineering (25%), hardware research and metrology certification (15%), team and general administration (12%), and go-to-market with the distribution companies and the Ministry (8%).

Hardware for large deployments in Years 3–5 is funded through asset-backed financing against signed distribution-company contracts, keeping the equity requirement small relative to the EGP 600-million-plus revenue it unlocks.

Risk & Governance

  • Procurement cycles — mitigated by a paid pilot with an audited recovery figure that converts into a framework tender.
  • Hardware capital at scale — hardware is sold at margin and large fleets are asset-financed against signed contracts.
  • Model trust — verdicts are explainable and human-reviewed; the models retrain on real utility data; thresholds are conservative.
  • Field integrity — nodes are sealed within the locked kiosk with tamper and offline alerts; only the antenna is exposed.
  • Data & privacy — the system meters transformers, not households; it localizes theft to a feeder for lawful field inspection.
Grid-Pulse is a robustness play: several independent layers of evidence, each degrading gracefully, combine into one accountable verdict. The result is higher confidence and fewer false accusations — the properties a utility requires before it will act.
