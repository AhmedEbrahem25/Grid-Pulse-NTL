Slide 3 · Why Now / The Hard Part

Not every loss is theft.

Technical loss — natural

Cables shed energy as heat. That loss rises with temperature and with load — Egyptian summer, peak hours.

Legitimate · no alarm

Non-technical loss — theft

Energy delivered, consumed, and never billed. Invisible in the totals, because it hides inside the natural loss.

Unexplained · must be flagged

A naive "meter-difference" alarm cannot tell them apart — so on a hot day it fires on physics, floods operators with false positives, and gets switched off.

Here is the part that makes this problem hard.

Measuring power is easy. Separating theft from legitimate loss is not.

Cables naturally shed energy as heat, and that technical loss rises with temperature and with load. In an Egyptian summer, at peak hours, natural loss can be large — and completely legitimate.

So a naive system that simply subtracts the meters from the transformer will raise an alarm on a hot day. It is firing on physics, not on theft.

That is why these systems flood operators with false positives — and a system that cries wolf gets ignored and switched off.

This is exactly where artificial intelligence becomes essential.

But The Grid Keeps Moving

  • Ambient temperature changes
  • Load profile changes hour by hour
  • Legitimate industrial customers distort the waveform
  • Feeder topology and demand change
Today's approaches fall into three categories:

manual inspection driven by tips and complaints,
fixed-threshold meter-difference alarms,
or monitoring platforms that only display data and leave the judgment to a human.

But the distribution grid is a living system.

Temperature changes. Load changes hour by hour. A legitimate registered factory distorts the current waveform exactly the way an illegal hook does. Feeder demand changes.

A fixed threshold cannot follow that. It is either too sensitive — and drowns the operator in false alarms — or too loose, and misses the theft entirely.
Slide 5 · Solution

Grid-Pulse NTL

An AI edge-system that catches theft — and knows the difference between theft and physics.

Continuous Energy Balance

AI Technical-Loss Baseline

Harmonic Fingerprint (1D-CNN)

Explainable Verdicts

Our solution is Grid-Pulse NTL.

A clip-on current sensor and an ESP32 edge node at the distribution transformer, feeding an AI detection pipeline.

It combines four things:

a continuous energy balance at the transformer — what goes out versus what the registered meters report;
an AI technical-loss baseline that predicts how much loss is normal right now, given load, temperature, and time;
a one-dimensional CNN that reads the harmonic fingerprint of the current;
and an explainable verdict layer.

Here is the whole idea in one sentence:

The AI predicts the natural loss, and only the unexplained excess — corroborated by power factor, harmonics, and a temporal anomaly — is flagged as theft.

That separation is the product. It is what kills the false positives.

An important clarification about scope.

Our system does not interfere with the operation of the grid itself. There is no rewiring and no interruption of supply. The sensor is a clip-on current transformer — it is installed without cutting the line.

We add a detection intelligence layer on top of the existing infrastructure.

At the edge, the ESP32 does the signal processing locally: RMS current, power factor, and an FFT for harmonic distortion. We ship features, not raw waveforms — tiny payloads, so this scales to thousands of transformers.

The ingest API validates every payload against a strict contract and rejects anything malformed. Redis absorbs bursts, and workers run the AI asynchronously.

Then five independent steps fuse into one verdict — and that verdict arrives with a probability and a list of reasons, not a bare alarm.
Slide 7 · Demo

Unregistered hook Theft · 94% · 5/5 reasons
This is the live console, and this is the demo — four beats.

Beat one: normal registered load. The energy balance holds. Verdict: normal.

Beat two: we raise the temperature. Measured loss rises — but the AI baseline rises with it, because that loss is explained by physics. Verdict: technical loss. No alarm. This is the false-positive killer.

Beat three: a registered industrial customer with a genuinely distorted waveform — high harmonic distortion. A threshold system would call this theft. Our harmonic CNN recognises a real factory. Verdict: normal.

Beat four: an unregistered hook on the network. Now the residual is unexplained, power factor collapses, harmonics match an illegal bypass, and the pattern is anomalous. The screen turns red: theft probability ninety-four percent, five out of five reasons met, roughly three kilowatts unaccounted for.

On six hundred held-out labelled scenarios, theft precision is one point zero — zero false positives — with F1 of zero point eight-eight, and zero percent false alarms on legitimate industrial load.

The beats replay in any order.

We priced it so that adoption carries almost no risk: the client pays a percentage of the energy we actually help recover, or a flat monthly subscription per transformer.

The numbers work. Hardware is roughly three hundred dollars per kiosk, against tens of thousands of pounds leaking every month from a single feeder. Return on investment is under one month per kiosk.

The market: around thirty billion pounds a year of non-technical loss in Egypt. Recovering even ten percent is three billion pounds.
