Grid-Pulse NTL

An AI edge-to-cloud system that detects electricity theft on the distribution grid — and knows the difference between real theft and the natural energy loss caused by heat and load, so it never cries wolf.

B2G · Climate & Infrastructure ESP32 · FastAPI · Redis · Celery · scikit-learn · PyTorch Demo verified in Docker Theft precision 1.00 · 0 false positives

01Executive summary

Electricity distribution companies in Egypt lose an estimated ~30 billion EGP per year to Non-Technical Loss (NTL) — energy that is generated, delivered, and consumed, but never billed. Illegal direct hooks, tampered meters, unlicensed workshops and brick factories, crypto rigs. Beyond the revenue, that unbilled demand overloads transformers and directly worsens load-shedding for the customers who do pay.

Measuring power is not the hard part. Separating theft from legitimate loss is. Cables naturally shed energy as heat, and that loss rises with temperature and with load — in an Egyptian summer at peak hours, natural loss is large and entirely legitimate. A naive "meter-difference" alarm fires on physics, floods operators with false positives, and gets switched off.

Grid-Pulse NTL runs a continuous energy balance at each distribution transformer, then uses an AI technical-loss baseline to explain away the natural loss. Only the unexplained excess — corroborated by a harmonic-signature classifier, a power-factor collapse, and a temporal anomaly — is flagged as theft, with a Theft Probability % and a five-reason checklist. That separation is the entire product.

The moat, in one sentence

An AI baseline predicts how much loss is normal right now from load, temperature and time. Only the unexplained excess — corroborated by power factor, harmonic shape, and a temporal anomaly — is flagged as theft, with a probability and a reasons checklist. That is what kills the false positives that make naive meter-difference alarms useless.

02The problem

2.1  What non-technical loss is

Every distribution transformer delivers energy to a set of registered, metered customers. The difference between what leaves the transformer and what the meters report is loss. That loss has two components:

  • Technical loss — physics. Copper loss in the conductors (∝ I²R), core loss in the transformer. Conductor resistance rises with temperature, so this loss grows on a hot day and under heavy load. It is unavoidable and entirely legitimate.
  • Non-technical loss (NTL) — theft. Illegal direct hooks upstream of the meter, tampered or bypassed meters, unregistered workshops and factories, unlicensed heavy loads. Energy delivered, consumed, never billed.

2.2  Why it is hard

Both components appear in exactly the same place — the gap between the transformer reading and the meter sum. A system that simply subtracts one from the other cannot tell them apart. On a 45 °C afternoon, technical loss can rise by 60% over its nominal value; a fixed threshold set to catch a 3 kW hook will fire on that heat every summer day.

The failure mode we exist to fix

A naive meter-difference alarm produces so many false positives that inspection teams stop trusting it. Once a lead is untrustworthy, the whole system is dead weight — inspectors go back to chasing tips, and most trips come back empty. False positives, not missed detections, are what kill NTL programmes in the field.

03What the system is

Grid-Pulse NTL is a non-invasive edge sensor plus an AI detection service plus an operations console. A clip-on current transformer and an ESP32 node sit inside the distribution kiosk on the low-voltage side. The node computes RMS current, real power, power factor and the harmonic spectrum locally, then posts a small JSON payload to the backend, which fuses six independent detection steps into one explainable verdict and pushes it live to the dashboard.

The three verdicts

LabelMeaningOperator action
NORMALThe energy balance holds — measured loss is within the expected natural bandMonitor
TECHNICAL_LOSSLoss is elevated, but the AI baseline explains it from temperature and load. Not an alarm.Monitor
NTL_THEFT_SUSPECTEDUnexplained excess loss, corroborated by independent evidence. Carries a probability, a watt estimate, a severity band and the reasons checklistDispatch field inspection

Design principles the code actually follows

  • The edge does the DSP. RMS, power factor and FFT/THD are computed on the ESP32, so the system ships features, not raw waveforms. Payloads are tiny, which is what makes thousands of transformers affordable.
  • Every model degrades to a rule. No trained artifact is mandatory. The technical-loss regressor falls back to a physics formula; the harmonic CNN falls back to a transparent THD/PF rule; the anomaly model returns a neutral 0. The system never fails closed because a .pkl is missing.
  • No verdict without an explanation. Every verdict carries a probability, a five-item reasons checklist with per-item detail strings, weighted XAI factors, and a recommended action.
  • Evidence layers may inform, never override. The energy-accounting layer adjusts the probability within a hard bound and can never flip a label or a checklist item.
  • Zero-trust ingest. Bearer token plus a strict schema with extra="forbid"; malformed payloads are rejected at the door, not downstream.

4.2  Two deployment modes, one codebase

INLINE_DETECTION=true (the default) runs the detection pipeline inside the API process — the simplest and most demo-safe path, with no dependency on a healthy worker. Set it to false and /ingest enqueues to Celery instead; the worker runs the identical detection code and publishes the verdict back over the Redis pub/sub channel verdicts, which the API relays to WebSocket clients. Same code, two operational stories.

06Detection pipeline

Six steps, each in its own module, fuse into one verdict. Steps ① ② ④ ⑤ are independent evidence sources; ③ is a bounded corroborating layer; ⑥ is the fusion.

StepModuleLOCRole
① Energy balanceenergy_balance.py10The raw mass balance at the transformer
② Technical-loss AItechnical_loss.py70Predicts the expected natural loss right now
③ Energy accountingaccounting.py70Rolling-window kWh evidence, bounded contribution
④ Harmonic signatureharmonic_cnn.py · harmonics.py145Illegal hook vs registered factory vs clean

6.1  Step ① — Energy balance

The starting point is deliberately trivial and auditable:

measured_loss_w = P_transformer_out − Σ P_registered_meters

A large positive value means energy is leaving the transformer that nobody is paying for. On its own it says nothing about why — that is step ②'s job. Everything downstream exists to interpret this one number.

6.2  Step ② — The AI technical-loss baseline

This is the false-positive killer. A RandomForest regressor predicts the natural loss for the current operating point across seven features:

FEATURES = ["load_w", "temp_c", "hour", "dow", "voltage_v", "current_a", "rated_kva"]

residual_w = measured_loss_w − expected_loss_w(features)

The residual — not the measured loss — is what the rest of the pipeline reasons about. When the day is hot, expected_loss_w rises with it, the residual stays near zero, and no alarm is raised.

The module computes a second prediction, expected_loss_at_nominal(), which re-runs the same features with temp_c = 25 °C. Comparing the two is how the system distinguishes "loss is high because it is hot" from "loss is high for no reason":

heat_inflated = expected_now > 1.15 * expected_nominal

If tech_loss.pkl is missing, a physics fallback stands in — copper loss proportional to load, resistance rising with temperature:

temp_factor = max(0.2, 1.0 + 0.03 * (temp_c − 25.0))
expected    = load_w * 0.03 * temp_factor      # ~3% of load at nominal temperature

6.3  Step ③ — Transformer energy accounting

An independent evidence module that works in energy (kWh over a rolling horizon) rather than instantaneous watts. It projects the current measured rate across the horizon, subtracts the registered energy and the AI-expected technical energy, and reports the leftover:

incoming_kwh   = load_w / 1000 * horizon_h            # horizon default 1.0 h
registered_kwh = registered_load_w / 1000 * horizon_h
excess_kwh     = (incoming_kwh − registered_kwh) − expected_technical_kwh
excess_pct     = excess_kwh / incoming_kwh * 100

contribution_pct = clamp(excess_pct * 1.4, −20, +18)

It also reports a stance — supporting, neutral or rejecting — and a confidence that grows with data sufficiency and with whether the trained regressor is actually loaded:

tech_loss_confidence = clamp(0.60 + 0.25*min(1, n/60) + (0.10 if model_loaded else 0), 0.5, 0.95)
Deliberately subordinate

This layer never produces a verdict or an alert by itself. Fusion folds it in as a bounded adjustment of at most +5 / −15 percentage points on the theft probability. It can damp a borderline case; it can never flip a label or change a checklist item.

6.4  Step ④ — Harmonic signature and power factor

Two complementary signals. First, a transparent scalar score combining power-factor collapse and excess distortion:

pf_term  = clamp((healthy_pf − pf) / healthy_pf)          # healthy_pf = 0.92
thd_term = clamp((thd_pct − thd_alert_pct) / 20)          # thd_alert = 10%
sig      = clamp(0.6*pf_term + 0.4*thd_term)

Second — and this is what makes the difference — a 1D convolutional network over the harmonic spectrum. High THD alone is ambiguous: a legitimate registered factory with variable-speed drives distorts the waveform just as badly as an illegal hook. A threshold cannot separate them; the shape of the spectrum can.

ClassHarmonic fingerprintEffect on the verdict
cleanNear-pure sine, tiny decaying harmonics, PF ≈ 1No harmonic evidence
legit_industrialCharacteristic 5th / 7th / 11th / 13th from PFC drives, low triplen (balanced three-phase)Clears a modest residual — factory is not flagged
illegal_bypassStrong 3rd + 5th + 7th + 9th, broad messy spread, low PF (half-wave / clipped loads)Corroborates theft

The classifier reads the 14-bin spectrum (h = 2…15) straight from the payload's harmonics[]. If an edge device only reports scalar THD, a synthesiser reconstructs a plausible spectrum, and empty bins are lifted to the 0.7 noise floor the network was trained with so a sparse vector is not out-of-distribution. If PyTorch or the artifact is unavailable, a transparent rule takes over: high THD with PF < 0.80 → illegal_bypass; high THD with healthy PF → legit_industrial.

6.5  Step ⑤ — Temporal anomaly

An IsolationForest over four features — [load_w, hour, residual_w, load_w − recent_avg] — catches repeating unexplained draws that a single-reading rule would miss, such as a workshop hook that runs 02:00–06:00. The raw decision_function is mapped so a clean reading sits well below the 0.5 decision point and a clear outlier saturates:

anom = clamp(0.5 − 4.0 * iforest.decision_function(x), 0, 1)

If the artifact is absent the score is 0 — neutral — so the balance and harmonic signals still drive the decision.

07Decision fusion & explainable AI

7.1  Classification

The core rule requires an unexplained residual plus at least one independent corroborating signal — or a residual so large it speaks for itself:

theft = (residual > margin and (sig > 0.30 or anom > 0.50)) or (residual > 2*margin)

# the CNN earns its place: a legit-industrial signature clears a *modest*
# unexplained residual (PFC lag, metering timing). A clear excess still trips.
if legit_industrial and residual < 3*margin:
    theft = False

With margin = RESIDUAL_ALERT_W = 400 W by default. If theft is false, the verdict is TECHNICAL_LOSS when the loss is heat-inflated and materially present, otherwise NORMAL:

if theft:                                              label = "NTL_THEFT_SUSPECTED"
elif heat_inflated and measured_loss > 0.5*expected_nominal: label = "TECHNICAL_LOSS"
else:                                                  label = "NORMAL"

7.2  Probability and severity

Confidence grows with the strength of the unexplained residual and is boosted when the two independent detectors corroborate it — three signals agreeing yields high confidence:

residual_strength = clamp(residual / (2*margin))
confidence = clamp(0.50 + 0.30*residual_strength + 0.14*sig + 0.10*anom, 0.55, 0.99)
theft_probability = round(confidence * 100)

# then the bounded accounting adjustment:
applied = clamp(round(evidence_weight * (contribution_pct − 18)), −15, +5)
theft_probability = clamp(theft_probability + applied, 55, 99)

Severity is set from the stolen fraction of load and the power factor:

SeverityCondition
criticaltheft fraction > 25% of load, or power factor < 0.75
hightheft fraction > 10%
mediumtheft fraction > 5%
lowtheft below 5%, or a TECHNICAL_LOSS verdict
infoNORMAL

7.3  The five-reason checklist

Every verdict — including the negative ones — ships a checklist, so an inspector sees why, not just what. Two of the five items carry deliberate gates that took real thought:

#ReasonCondition in codeWhy it is gated that way
1Energy imbalanceresidual > marginThe raw unexplained watts
2Expected loss exceededmeasured > 1.15*expected and residual > marginPercent over the AI baseline, not over a constant
3Power factor droppedpf < healthy_pfIndependent of the energy math
4THD increasedthd > alert and not (legit_industrial and residual ≤ margin)High THD only counts as theft evidence when the harmonic shape is illegal. A registered factory whose balance holds does not tick this box

08Machine learning

8.1  Technical-loss regressor

Trained on 8,000 physics-seeded synthetic rows. The generator samples a realistic operating envelope and computes the target from the copper-loss relation, with a mild peak-hour effect and 5% noise:

VariableDistribution
load_wUniform 500 – 12,000 W
temp_cUniform 10 – 48 °C
voltage_vNormal(230, 4)
current_aDerived: load / voltage
rated_kvaChoice of 250 / 315 / 400 / 630
hour, dowUniform integers; hours 12–17 add a +5% loss effect
Targetload * 0.03 * max(0.2, 1 + 0.03*(temp − 25)), × (1 + peak + 𝒩(0, 0.05))

Model: RandomForestRegressor(n_estimators=120, max_depth=14).

The model is genuinely multi-factor

A reviewer warned that a temperature-only model reads as simplistic. The learned feature importances settle it — load leads, not temperature:

8.2  Temporal anomaly model

IsolationForest(n_estimators=150, contamination=0.02) over [load_w, hour, residual_w, load_w − recent_avg]. A subtlety worth noting: the training residuals are seeded with realistic jitter (𝒩(0, 90 W) residual noise, 𝒩(0, 120 W) load wobble) rather than exact zeros. Without that, the model treats every non-zero residual as an outlier and the anomaly signal becomes useless noise.

8.3  Harmonic 1D-CNN

Input is a 14-bin spectrum (h = 2…15, magnitude as % of fundamental), z-normalised with the per-bin mean and standard deviation stored in harmonic_meta.json. Architecture:

Conv1d(1→8, k=3) → ReLU → Conv1d(8→16, k=3) → ReLU
  → AdaptiveAvgPool1d(1) → Flatten → Linear(16→24) → ReLU → Linear(24→3)

Trained on 6,000 balanced synthetic spectra across the three classes, whose generators encode genuine power-engineering structure — characteristic 6k±1 harmonics with low triplen for drives, strong triplen with a broad messy spread for clipped/half-wave illegal loads. Reported validation accuracy: 1.00 on the held-out split.

Read that number correctly

A validation accuracy of 1.00 reflects that the three synthetic classes are cleanly separable by construction. It is evidence that the network learns the intended shape distinction — it is not a claim about real field spectra. See §9.4 and §15.

9.3  Why precision is favoured over recall

The 34 missed thefts are a deliberate trade, not an accident. An inspection trip costs money and inspector goodwill; a lead that fires must be trustworthy or the programme dies (§2.2). A missed stealthy hook stays in the system and will very likely trip on a later reading as its draw grows or its pattern repeats — the temporal anomaly detector exists partly for that. A false positive, by contrast, burns trust immediately and permanently. Recall is recoverable over time; precision is not.

9.4  Honest limitations of these numbers

State this proactively

All 600 scenarios are synthetic, generated from the same physical model family the detector reasons about. That makes them a valid test of internal consistency, decision-boundary behaviour, and the false-positive thesis — but not a claim of field accuracy. Real feeder data will contain effects this generator does not model: unbalanced three-phase loading, metering clock skew, capacitor-bank switching transients, harmonic resonance, and theft methods with no distinctive harmonic signature at all. The recall of 0.78 is disclosed rather than hidden, and the single most valuable next step is any slice of real utility data.

10.2  Persistence

SQLModel over SQLite by default (sqlite:////data/gridpulse.db on a named Docker volume), Postgres-ready through DATABASE_URL. Theft verdicts can fan out to a webhook and/or Telegram. Both are best-effort and no-op when unconfigured — a slow or unreachable sink can never block ingest or stall the demo.

11.1  Sensing chain

The proof-of-concept node is an ESP32 with an SCT-013-000 clip-on current transformer (100 A / 50 mA, no internal burden) and an optional ZMPT101B voltage sensor. The CT is current-output, so it needs conditioning before the ADC:

Post period1,000 msOne payload per second per node
Why the FFT sample rate matters

2,560 Hz over a 256-point frame is exactly 5 cycles of a 50 Hz fundamental, so the fundamental lands on an integer bin and harmonics h = 2…15 land on bins 10, 15, 20 … cleanly. Choosing the sample rate to be coherent with the line frequency is what makes the harmonic vector trustworthy enough for the CNN to classify.

11.2  What the node computes and sends

The firmware runs EmonLib calcVI() for RMS voltage, RMS current, real power, apparent power and power factor, then a windowed FFT on the current for THD and the harmonic vector. It builds the strict-contract JSON and POSTs it with the bearer token. Everything heavy stays at the edge: the backend receives roughly a few hundred bytes per second per transformer instead of a waveform stream.

Optional additions the config already anticipates: a DHT22 for real ambient temperature (which feeds the AI baseline directly — a fixed TEMP_C is used otherwise), and a swap of Wi-Fi for LoRaWAN or NB-IoT in production.

11.3  Production upgrade path

The POC clip-on CT becomes Rogowski coils on the LV busbars inside a sealed distribution kiosk (RMU), with the ESP32 in an IP65 enclosure and the antenna routed outside the metal cabinet's Faraday cage. This is a low-voltage-side-only, non-invasive retrofit: no medium-voltage contact, no galvanic connection, zero supply interruption, fully reversible. That property is what makes a utility willing to authorise installation at all.

Replay-safe

The beats can be run in any order, any number of times. 13Deployment & operations

13.1  One command

cp .env.example .env
docker-compose up --build     # api :8000 + redis + celery worker
python ml/train.py            # optional — the physics fallback works untrained
# open http://localhost:8000

Three services: redis:7-alpine with a healthcheck, the api running uvicorn, and the Celery worker. Both Python services mount ./ml/artifacts at /app/artifacts so a retrained model is picked up without a rebuild, and share the named volume gpdata at /data for the SQLite database.

13.2  Field installation

  1. Open the distribution kiosk (RMU) on the low-voltage side only.
  2. Clip the Rogowski coils / CTs around the LV busbars — no conductor is cut, no supply is interrupted.
  3. Mount the ESP32 node in its IP65 enclosure; route the antenna outside the metal cabinet.
  4. Set DEVICE_ID, TRANSFORMER_ID, backend URL and token in config.h; calibrate against a known load.
  5. Register the transformer's metered customers so the backend has a registered-load figure to balance against.

The installation is fully reversible — nothing about it is permanent or destructive, which is what makes the authorisation conversation with a utility tractable.

13.3  Scaling considerations

  • Bandwidth — a few hundred bytes per second per node; a 10,000-node fleet is a modest ingest load, and TinyML on-device inference (§16) would cut it by ~99% by transmitting only on suspicion.
  • Compute — detection is milliseconds per reading; Celery workers scale horizontally behind Redis.
  • Storage — switch DATABASE_URL to Postgres; the schema is already SQLModel.
  • Thresholds — RESIDUAL_ALERT_W, HEALTHY_PF and THD_ALERT_PCT should become per-feeder rather than global once real data establishes local baselines.

15Risks & limitations

15.1  Strengths worth stating plainly

  • The false-positive thesis is implemented and measured, not asserted on a slide — 0 false positives, 0.0% on legitimate industrial load.
  • Explainability is structural, not cosmetic: probability, gated reasons checklist, weighted factors, recommended action on every verdict.
  • The technical-loss model is genuinely multi-factor (load 0.58 > temperature 0.27 > current 0.15), and the harmonic CNN is load-bearing rather than decorative.
  • End-to-end completeness — firmware, contract, API, AI, persistence, dashboard, evaluation harness, deployment engineering, and the business model.
  • Every model degrades to a transparent rule, so nothing about the system is a black box that can fail silently.

15.2  Risks, stated honestly

#RiskSeverityMitigation / status
1All evaluation is synthetic. No real feeder data has touched the pipelineHighDisclose proactively; treat any slice of real utility data as the top priority. Phase 1 of GTM exists precisely to produce it

15.3  Questions a judge is likely to ask

QuestionAnswer
"Isn't this just a temperature threshold?"No — seven features, and load dominates the learned importances at 0.58 versus temperature at 0.27. The temperature comparison is a second prediction at nominal, used only to explain away loss, never to trigger an alarm.
"What if a legitimate factory has terrible THD?"That is test class 3 and demo beat 3. The 1D-CNN classifies the harmonic shape; a registered factory scores 0.0% false-alarm rate across 139 cases.
"Why is recall only 0.78?"Deliberate. Inspection trips cost money and trust; a lead that fires must be right. Missed stealthy hooks recur and get caught later — a false positive is permanent damage. See §9.3.
"Does it work without the trained models?"Yes. Physics fallback for the baseline, a transparent rule for the harmonics, neutral score for the anomaly. Degraded, but functional and still explainable.
"Do you need smart meters?"No. It works on the infrastructure that exists — that is the wedge against AMI-dependent approaches.
"Is the data real?"No, and we say so. Synthetic, physics-seeded, held-out. Real feeder data is the explicit next step.
