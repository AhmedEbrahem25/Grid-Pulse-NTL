The target in the field is a sealed distribution kiosk (RMU) — an iron box carrying thousands of amps. We don't bolt anything to its shell: we retrofit it from the inside, non-invasively, on the low-voltage side only. No cables cut, no outage, no one near the 11 kV.

01

Inside the iron box — three chambers

The kiosk is partitioned. We stay out of the dangerous half entirely and work only where the street-level power leaves — the low-voltage panel.

◆ Never touched

MV chamber

Incoming 11 kV feeders from the primary substation. Lethal, sealed, and completely outside our workflow. We never open or instrument this side.

● Pass-through

The transformer

Steps 11 kV down to 380/220 V. We measure its output, not its internals — the transformer is simply the source of the current we watch.

● Our zone

LV panel

Thick copper busbars carry hundreds–thousands of amps out to the streets and workshops. This is where energy balance is measured — and where theft downstream shows up.

02

The sensor grows up

The bench clamp proves the physics on a lamp and a hair-dryer. It can't wrap a 2,000-amp busbar — so production swaps it for a flexible coil that can.

Proof of concept

SCT-013-000 clamp

13 mm aperture, 100 A. Perfect on the bench; too small for a busbar.

  • Non-invasive around a single cable
  • Cheap, instant to demo
  • Reads the lamp-vs-dryer theft scenario
Role: prove the detection math end-to-end.
Production

Rogowski coil ×3

A flexible "rope CT" — the technician just wraps it around the busbar and clips it shut. One per phase (L1/L2/L3).

  • Fits any busbar or fat cable, in tight panels
  • Reads up to ~5,000 A with a safe low-level output
  • No cables disconnected — zero outage
  • Alt: industrial hinged split-core CT
Role: kiosk-grade sensing on live busbars.

04

Why non-invasive wins the contract

A utility can't accept anything that risks an outage or a technician. Every design choice here removes a reason to say no.

Deployment guarantees

  • Never enters the MV side — all sensing is on the 380/220 V LV panel.
  • No galvanic connection — Rogowski coils couple magnetically; nothing is wired into the live conductor.
  • Zero service interruption — no feeder is disconnected to install.
  • Reversible — unclip the coils and the kiosk is exactly as it was.
  • Tamper-resistant — sealed inside the locked kiosk, only the antenna is exposed.

This is the difference between a science-fair gadget and something the Ministry of Electricity can roll out across 90,000 kiosks. The physics is the same as the bench demo — a current transformer and an energy balance — but the packaging is built for the iron box in the photo.
